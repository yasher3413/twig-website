---
title: Tabs and sleeping tabs
description: How twig keeps lots of tabs open without paying for all of them.
---

Most browsers keep every tab running as a full, live page, so memory grows with every tab you open. twig assumes the opposite: a tab you aren't looking at shouldn't cost you anything.

## Sleeping tabs

A tab you haven't looked at for **10 minutes** falls asleep. Its webview is torn down, but twig keeps its title, address and scroll position, so it wakes where you left it when you click it.

Only a few tabs stay awake at once. Choose how many in **Settings → Memory**:

| Setting | Awake at once |
| --- | --- |
| Frugal | 3 |
| Balanced (default) | 5 |
| Roomy | 10 |

A sleeping tab shows a grey dot, and its icon and title dim.

## Tabs that never sleep

A tab that's busy is left alone, even if that puts you over the limit:

- it has text you've typed but not sent;
- it's playing audio or video;
- it's using your camera or microphone.

## Tabs that cost nothing

A new tab holds no webview until you load something in it, and neither does a restored one. Reopening a few hundred tabs after a restart costs about what reopening one does.

## The memory readout

**Settings → Memory** shows a live ledger:

- **in use**: what twig is using now;
- **awake** and **asleep**: how many tabs are in each state;
- **not spent**: an estimate of what the sleeping tabs would cost if they were awake, at the current average.

## Closing tabs

Closed tabs are archived and searchable in **Library → Closed**, not just reopenable with ⇧⌘T. Pages you read are also kept by [Recall](/docs/recall), so closing a tab doesn't mean losing it.

## Other tab basics

- Drag tabs to reorder them.
- **⌘1–⌘9** jumps to a tab; **⇧⌘[** and **⇧⌘]** move between them.
- **⌘B** hides or shows the tab strip.
- Per-site zoom is remembered across restarts.
