title: Checkpoints
description: Save a named snapshot of a space, browse it on a timeline, and resume or fork it later.
---

A checkpoint is a named snapshot of a space: its tabs, their order and its split view, with an optional note.

## Save a checkpoint

Press **⇧⌘S**, give it a name, and add a note if you like. Later checkpoints remember which checkpoint they came from.

## Browse checkpoints

Press **⇧⌘H** to open the timeline. Search it, and preview each checkpoint's saved tab order and split view before you open anything.

## Resume or fork

- **Resume** opens a checkpoint in a new space. Your current work stays where it is.
- **Fork** does the same with a new name, so you can explore another direction from that point and keep both.

Only the active tab and its split partner wake when a checkpoint opens; the rest stay asleep until you use them. Reading positions are restored where possible.

## What a checkpoint keeps, and doesn't

A checkpoint keeps addresses and layout. It doesn't keep old versions of websites, form contents or sign-in state. Restoring a reading position is best-effort on pages that load or rearrange content as you scroll.

Checkpoints don't change as you keep browsing. They're saved on your Mac, excluded from private windows, and you can delete any of them without closing open tabs.

## Package it

Choose **Package this research** on a checkpoint to turn it into a [research package](/docs/research-packages).
